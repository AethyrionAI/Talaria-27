Privacy Policy

Talaria for iOS · Effective: 2026-08-10

The short version: Talaria has no servers. We — the app's developer — do not collect, receive, store, sell, or share any of your data. There are no accounts, no analytics, and no tracking. Your conversations are processed either on your iPhone or on a computer you own and operate. The only parties that ever handle your data are Apple's on-device frameworks and the services you yourself configure.

Who we are

Talaria is an iOS client for a personal AI assistant. It is developed by James Jones. This policy covers the Talaria iOS app and this website.

The two ways Talaria works

On-device (the default). Out of the box, Talaria's assistant runs entirely on your iPhone using Apple's on-device foundation models. Your messages, attachments, and the assistant's replies are processed and stored on your device. Nothing is sent to us — or to anyone.

Paired with your own server (optional). You can connect Talaria to a Hermes agent running on your own computer. In that mode, the content you send — messages, attachments, voice, and any sensor data you explicitly enable — travels over your own private network (for example, a Tailscale tailnet you operate) to your own machine. That machine, and any AI model providers you have configured on it under your own accounts, process your data under your control and those providers' own terms. We are not a party to any of it.

What the app stores on your device

Conversation history, attachments you add, settings, and pairing credentials (kept in the iOS Keychain) are stored locally on your iPhone. Deleting the app deletes this local data. Talaria does not sync your data to iCloud or to any cloud service of ours.

Voice

Talaria's default voice mode uses Apple's on-device speech recognition and synthesis. When paired with your own server, you can optionally use a realtime voice engine: in that mode your audio is streamed through your server to the voice provider you configured there (for example, OpenAI's realtime API under your own API key). This is off unless you configure it yourself on your own server. When a voice session is running on that realtime engine, the app shows an indicator on the voice screen for the whole session, so you can tell at the time that your audio is leaving the phone.

Permissions, and why each is asked for

PermissionUsed for
Microphone & Speech RecognitionVoice conversations and dictation. Default processing is on-device.
Photos / CameraAttaching images you choose to your messages.
Calendars & RemindersLetting the assistant read and create events and reminders when you ask it to.
ContactsLetting the assistant look up people you reference, on your request.
LocationAnswering location-based requests (including weather via Apple's WeatherKit, which receives your approximate location to return a forecast).
Health (HealthKit)Only if you opt in while paired: sending the health metrics you select to your own server. Health data is never sent anywhere else, and never to us.
Face IDThe optional App Lock. Biometric matching happens entirely inside iOS; the app only learns "unlocked" or "not."
Motion & FitnessOnly if you opt in while paired: activity context for your own server.

Permissions are optional; each feature degrades gracefully without its grant. Sensor sharing to a paired server is governed by a single explicit switch, off by default.

What we receive: nothing

The app contains no analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting SDKs, and makes no network connections to infrastructure we operate — because we operate none. If you choose to share diagnostics with developers via iOS settings, Apple may provide us aggregate crash reports through App Store Connect under Apple's privacy terms.

Third parties, exhaustively

  • Apple — on-device models, Speech, WeatherKit, HealthKit, and (if it ships) Private Cloud Compute, each under Apple's privacy commitments.
  • Your own server and the providers you configured on it — under your control and their terms, not ours.
  • This website is hosted on GitHub Pages and loads fonts from Google Fonts; both services receive standard request logs (IP address, user agent). The site itself sets no cookies and runs no analytics.

Your data, your controls

Delete a conversation in the app to remove it locally; delete the app to remove everything local; unpair to stop all traffic to your server. Data on your own server is yours to manage there. Since we hold nothing, there is nothing for you to request from us — but questions are welcome at the address below.

Children

Talaria is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone — children included, by construction.

Changes

If the app's data practices ever change, this policy will be updated before the change ships, with the effective date above revised.
